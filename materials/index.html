      Print

      Two things to print before the first lesson. The chip labels are a must. The chip maps are strongly recommended.

      Must print

      Chip labels

      The numbers printed on real chips are tiny and hard to read, even for adults. A label puts a big, clear number on top of each chip, so builders can find the right chip at a glance and follow the video.

      1. Print on letter-size full-sheet sticker paper, at 100% ("Actual size", not "Fit to page").
      2. Cut along the thin grey lines. A paper trimmer makes this quick.
      3. Stick one label on top of each chip, leaving the chip's notched end showing.

      Chip labels PDF · Letter
      Recommended

      Chip maps

      A map of each chip shows what every leg does. On paper, builders can hold it, point at it and keep it right next to the breadboard. Handling real paper helps more than flipping back and forth on a screen.

      1. Print both pages in color. Card stock lasts longest.
      2. Cut the four maps apart. Laminate them if you will reuse them.

      Pop-up events

      A Glitch'n pop-up is a drop-in event in a public place, like a library. Visitors decide how far to go. We set up the room in three zones, each marked with a poster, so people can see where to start and move deeper when they're ready.

      • Hang outWatch, listen and get comfortable. Seats and a speaker playing finished circuits.
      • Mess aroundFinished circuits on tables. Visitors push buttons, turn knobs and cover light sensors, prompted by the table signs.
      • Geek outBuild tables with kits and screens, where visitors build a circuit from scratch with a helper.

      Checklist for a typical pop-up

      For two or three hours in one room. Scale it to your space.

      • Posters: Welcome at the door; Hang out, Mess around and Geek out, one per zone; Feeling curious? near the build tables; Before you go at the exit.
      • Table signs: Turn a knob, Push a button and Cover a sensor next to the matching finished circuits; Try something new on the build tables.
      • Finished circuits: three to six, built ahead of time from kits, for the Mess around tables. Each needs a speaker.
      • Build kits: one per seat at the Geek out tables, with chip labels and chip maps. Count these in your kits above.
      • Screens: a tablet or laptop at each build seat, with the first lesson open.
      • Hand sanitizer on every table; the table signs ask visitors to use it.
      • Stickers: one per visitor to take home.
      • Helpers: at least one at the build tables. Visitors stay longer when someone builds alongside them.

      Posters

      24 × 36 in. Take the PDFs to a copy shop or print service and ask for 24 × 36 in posters, trimmed: the files include bleed and crop marks. Mounting them on foam board helps them stand on an easel.
